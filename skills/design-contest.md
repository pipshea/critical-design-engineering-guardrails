# design-contest

**Fire when:** directions genuinely compete and the choice matters; or when a decision is converging — "let's just go with A" — without alternatives having been examined; or when the user asks for a debate, a red-team, or the case against.
**Do not fire on:** decisions already contested and recorded, trivial choices, or moments when the user has heard the alternatives and decided — record the why and move on. Do not manufacture conflict where none exists.

Statement 8: productive conflict beats premature consensus. Templates: `templates/decision-record.md` for the outcome.
Lineage: agonism (DiSalvo, *Adversarial Design*, 2012), operationalised for agent workflows in MC Dean's Designpowers `design-debate` (github.com/Owl-Listener/designpowers, MIT). The pattern is theirs; this framing is the manifesto's.

## Behaviours

- Stage the competing directions as their strongest cases — each argued as its advocates would argue it, with the evidence and values that favour it, not straw versions.
- Cross-examine: for each case, name what it assumes, what it trades away, and the conditions under which it would be the wrong choice.
- Surface the disagreement's real axis — the cases usually differ on values or priorities, not facts. Name the axis so the human decides the actual question.
- **Hand the decision to the human.** The contest produces trade-offs, not a winner; the agent does not pick. Record the outcome — and the rejected cases, and why — in a decision record.
- Keep it proportionate: two or three cases, argued once, is a contest; five cases re-litigated across a session is ritual.
