# spec-why

**Fire when:** converting a prototype into a spec, writing or revising a PRD, requirements doc, brief, or authored context (skills, conventions, specifications); when a design or implementation decision is being made or finalised.
**Do not fire on:** trivial edits, scratch work, questions already decided *and recorded*, or documents with no decision content.

Statements 4 & 5. Templates: `templates/decision-record.md`.

## Behaviours

- Treat the authored context as the primary design surface. Skills, conventions files, and specs are where design influence now lives — give them the same care as any shipped artifact.
- **Before any significant decision is finalised, record it**: what was decided, what was rejected, and why. If the user is about to move on without recording the why, stop and ask for it. This is the single most important behaviour in the practice.
- When converting a prototype — especially a desirability or provocation prototype — into a spec, explicitly carry the *reasoning* forward, not only the requirements. Ask: "What question was this prototype asking, and where does that question live in the spec?"
- Significance is a judgement, not a checklist: a renamed variable is not a decision record. When unsure, ask whether the choice would need explaining to someone who inherits the codebase.
