# Influences — The Critical Design Engineering Manifesto

Thinkers whose work shaped the manifesto's statements, with the statement each informed. Alphabetical by surname; close collaborators folded into the lead entry.

---

Bainbridge, Lisanne: "Ironies of Automation" (1983); the paradox that judgement matters more as making automates. Statement 5.

Bleecker, Julian: "Design Fiction" (2009); objects that tell stories about possible futures. Statement 8.

Boer, Laurens & Donovan, Jared: "Provotypes" (2012); provocation over evaluation. Statement 8.

Chalmers, Matthew (and Ian MacColl): seamful design (2003); revealing rather than hiding the seams. Statement 3.

Chun, Wendy Hui Kyong: *Programmed Visions* (2011); "sourcery," source code mistaken for transparency. Statement 2.

Cross, Nigel: "Designerly Ways of Knowing" (1982); design as a distinct epistemology. Statement 0.

Dean, MC: *Designpowers* (2026; github.com/Owl-Listener/designpowers); a design workflow built entirely from markdown skills and agent definitions. Authored context as the material that steers agent behaviour. Statement 4.

DiSalvo, Carl: *Adversarial Design* (2012); agonism, making conflict contestable. Statements 8 and 10.

Dunne, Anthony & Raby, Fiona: *Speculative Everything* (2013); design that reinforces how things are versus design that poses a question. Statement 8.

Fuller, Matthew: *Behind the Blip: Essays on the Culture of Software* (2003); "critical software" that runs and critiques at once. Statement 8.

Fuller, Matthew (ed.): *Software Studies: A Lexicon* (2008); software as an object of cultural and critical analysis. The field the manifesto works within.

Galloway, Alexander R.: *The Interface Effect* (2012); the interface as threshold and generative friction rather than transparent window. Statement 3.

Krug, Steve: *Don't Make Me Think* (2000); the seamless experience orthodoxy the manifesto pushes back against. Statement 3.

Lessig, Lawrence: *Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace* (1999; rev. as *Code: Version 2.0*, 2006); code as an instrument of governance. Statement 9.

Löwgren, Jonas & Stolterman, Erik: *Thoughtful Interaction Design* (2004); the digital as design material. Statement 0.

Mackenzie, Adrian: *Wirelessness* (2010); technology as a distributed field of relations rather than a discrete device. Statement 6.

Narayanan, Arvind & Kapoor, Sayash: "AI as Normal Technology" (2025) and "Why AI hasn't replaced software engineers, and won't" (2026); the decide-execute-deliver sandwich, value migrating upward. Statements 4, 5, 10.

Nygard, Michael: the Architecture Decision Record (2011); the why as its own artifact. Statement 5.

Oliver, Julian; Savičić, Gordan; Vasiliev, Danja: "The Critical Engineering Manifesto" (2011). The source text; structure, numbering, cadence, and its FOSS culture commitments.

Pope, Richard: "Designing the seams, not seamless design" (2025); transparency at the point of use. Statement 3.

Prado, Luiza & Oliveira, Pedro: "Cheat Sheet for a Non- (or Less-) Colonialist Speculative Design" (2014); whose futures speculative design imagines, and whose it does not. Statement 8.

Rittel, Horst: design rationale and the IBIS method (1970); the why as capturable, not merely implicit. Statement 5.

Schön, Donald: *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983); the back-talk of materials. Statement 0.

Star, Susan Leigh (with Karen Ruhleder): infrastructure and "when is infrastructure" (1996, 1999). Statements 7 and 9.

Suchman, Lucy: *Plans and Situated Actions* (1987), *Human-Machine Reconfigurations* (2007); plans as resources for action, not determinants of it; the human-machine boundary as continually reconfigured. Statements 4 and 6.

Ullman, Ellen: *Close to the Machine* (1997); code as intimacy and seduction. Statement 0.

Weiser, Mark (and John Seely Brown): "The Coming Age of Calm Technology" (1996); "beautiful seams." Statement 3.

Winner, Langdon: "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" (1980); design choices legislate. Statement 9.

---

**Field source.** Statement 5's wording was also shaped by the "Spec is the source" session at AI Engineer Melbourne (2026) — Gillies' point that code captures a decision's outcome, not the decision itself. Conference reportage rather than peer-reviewed work; cite as a field source.

**Note on Statement 1.** The capability-and-threat framing has no single named source. Nearest candidate if grounding is wanted: Narayanan & Kapoor's dual-use framing.
